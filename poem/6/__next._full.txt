The beloved's constant questioning of the lover's identity or worth ("tu kya hai") is perceived as dismissive and insulting. The lover's rhetorical counter-question, "You tell me, what kind of conversation is this?", highlights the absurdity and harshness of the beloved's behavior, suggesting that such an exchange is devoid of love, respect, or true engagement.

Ghalib masterfully uses a common conversational phrase to convey deep emotional pain and frustration. The repeated "kya hai" (what is it?) from both sides underscores a fundamental misunderstanding or a power imbalance in the relationship. The beloved's question challenges the lover's very existence or significance, while the lover's retort questions the nature of their interaction itself, implying that it's neither fair nor loving.

This couplet also introduces a theme of self-worth and recognition. The lover feels belittled and invalidated by the beloved's constant interrogation. The rhetorical question, "Someone tell me what that mischievous, fierce-tempered one is!", further emphasizes the beloved's elusive and enigmatic qualities.
