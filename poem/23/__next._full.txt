10:T6fb,This iconic couplet by Ghalib encapsulates a profound philosophical stance on faith, reality, and human solace. The first line, "Hum ko maloom hai jannat ki haqeeqat lekin" (We know the reality of paradise, but), immediately sets up a dichotomy between objective knowledge and subjective experience. The speaker, often understood as Ghalib himself, claims to possess a deeper understanding of paradise—perhaps implying its transient nature, its metaphorical significance, or simply a disillusionment with conventional religious promises. The "lekin" (but) signals a pivot, introducing the second, more humanistic, perspective.

The second line, "Dil ke khush rakhne ko Ghalib ye khayal achha hai" (To keep the heart happy, Ghalib, this thought is good), reveals the practical wisdom of the poet. Despite his perceived knowledge of reality, Ghalib acknowledges the comfort and psychological benefit that the *idea* of paradise provides to the human heart. It's not about the factual existence or non-existence of paradise, but about the solace, hope, and peace that the concept brings to troubled souls. This line also includes Ghalib's *takhallus* (pen name), a common feature in the final couplet of a ghazal, reinforcing its standalone nature.

The couplet masterfully balances skepticism with empathy. It's a testament to Ghalib's ability to question established beliefs while simultaneously appreciating the human need for comforting narratives. He doesn't dismiss paradise outright but rather places its utility in the realm of emotional well-being.
